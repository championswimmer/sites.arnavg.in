Stop two: the economics. If every open chat holds its own always-on VM, cost scales with open sessions, not useful work. The receipt on the right is the headline: two thousand 4-gig VMs, always on, roughly fifty-six thousand dollars a month in compute alone, before disks and egress. The next three slides derive that number and show where it burns.

The receipt slide counted RAM only. Here is the rest.

CPUs: two vCPUs per session, ~$50 a month each — the biggest line.

RAM: four gigs per session, ~$28 — the $56k you already saw.

Disks: forty gigs of checkout per session, ~$4 — small each, $8k at fleet scale. Total ~$164k/mo.

This slide must not repeat the receipt — it dissects it. Click through CPU, then RAM, then disk. Per session, always-on: about fifty dollars of vCPU, twenty-eight of RAM, four of disk — roughly eighty-two dollars per session per month. Multiply by four sessions per user: about three hundred twenty-eight dollars per user. Multiply by five hundred users: on the order of one hundred sixty-four thousand a month. The punchline: the fifty-six thousand on the previous slide was RAM alone. CPUs roughly double it again, and disks add a third line item nobody budgets.

Worse: sessions sit paused-by-you, billed-by-them overnight and all weekend.

At 75% idle, ~$42k of the $56k RAM line is waiting money — before nights and weekends.

Advance the hour bar first: a few minutes of real compute, then long stretches of the human reading, thinking, reviewing diffs — and the VM bills through all of it, from launch until stopped. Then reveal the week bar: roughly ten active hours against a hundred-and-sixty-eight-hour week. Nights, weekends, holidays — sessions nobody closed keep billing because nothing parked them. At seventy-five percent idle inside working hours, about forty-two of the fifty-six thousand RAM line is waiting money — and the overnight and weekend tail makes it worse. The fix is not cheaper VMs; it is not paying for waiting at all.

the fix, in one sentence

Pay for the seconds your VM runs

🅿️

Park it cheap

paused state → cheap storage
~$0.02 / GiB-mo

Pay while it computes

CPU + RAM only while active
per-second metering

Pausing does not make compute free — the snapshot still sits on storage. But parked state costs cents per gig-month while idle RAM costs dollars. So the goal shape is serverless: pay storage prices to park, pay compute prices only while the agent actually runs. The next section shows the machinery that makes both halves possible.
