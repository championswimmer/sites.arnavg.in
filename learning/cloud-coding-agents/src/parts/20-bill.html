Stop two: the economics. If every open chat holds its own always-on VM, cost scales with open sessions, not useful work. The receipt on the right is the headline: two thousand 4-gig VMs, always on, roughly fifty-six thousand dollars a month in compute alone, before disks and egress. The next three slides derive that number and show where it burns.

Click through the equation. Five hundred developers times three to four concurrent sessions is fifteen hundred to two thousand live sandboxes — the speaker rounds up to thousands. Floor pricing: a 4-gig cloud VM is about thirty dollars a month, roughly seven dollars per gig-month. Four gigs is the plausible minimum for an agent with a repo, language server and tools. The table is illustrative compute-only, before disks, egress and logging — and every figure assumes always-on.

At 75% idle, ~$42k of the $56k is waiting money.

Advance the bar segment by segment: a few minutes of real compute, then long stretches of the human reading, thinking, reviewing diffs, switching tasks — and the VM bills through all of it, from launch until stopped. At seventy-five percent idle, about forty-two of the fifty-six thousand is waiting money. The fix is not cheaper VMs; it is not paying for waiting at all.

the fix, in one sentence

Pausing does not make compute free — the snapshot still sits on storage. But parked state costs cents per gig-month while idle RAM costs dollars. So the goal shape is serverless: pay storage prices to park, pay compute prices only while the agent actually runs. The next section shows the machinery that makes both halves possible.
