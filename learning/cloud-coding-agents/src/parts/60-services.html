The side-project loop, end to end.

Create: a Linux VM inside your environment.

It reaches your database over the private network.

Checkpoint once you like the setup. Templates cache it.

Then fan out. Destroy when done; the meter stops.

Walk the four clicks. Create gives you an isolated Linux VM scoped to your Railway environment — Docker, coding agents and CLIs come preinstalled, and you can even SSH in. Reach back is the point most people miss: flip on private networking and the sandbox talks to your real Postgres, Redis and internal APIs. Your agent tests against reality, not mocks. Checkpoint saves that working setup as a named bootable disk snapshot; templates cache the recipe for about a week. The documented example fans out one sandbox per agent from a repo-ready checkpoint. Fan out is the loop: one sandbox per task, agent, test or worker — fifty at once on Hobby, a hundred on Pro per environment — metered per second on CPU and memory actually used, and destroying one stops billing cold. Say the honest trade out loud: checkpoints preserve disk, not live RAM. For side projects that is exactly the right trade.

Let the audience click cards; details appear below. Step 1 lights up the RAM-preserving row: E2B pauses the live VM with memory included and resumes in about a second, paused free; Daytona VM sandboxes preserve memory on pause and bill disk-only while parked; Fly suspends the whole VM including RAM and wakes in hundreds of milliseconds. Step 2 covers snapshot-restart: Vercel stop auto-snapshots the filesystem with tiered timeouts; Cloudflare backs directories up to R2 with a 3-day default TTL and restores them as an overlay; Modal snapshots become restorable clones. Step 3 points back: Railway did checkpoint and fork per environment on the previous slide — start there tonight.

Two clicks. While running, everyone meters CPU plus RAM per second, plus creations and egress — that is the same everywhere. While parked, the bill collapses: E2B paused sandboxes are free, Daytona and Fly charge disk only, Vercel, Cloudflare and Modal charge snapshot or backup storage, and a destroyed Railway sandbox bills nothing at all. That collapse is the whole business case: the waiting money from section two goes nearly to zero.
